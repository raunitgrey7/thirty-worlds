What makes a haiku

In English practice, a haiku is three unrhymed lines of 5, 7 and 5 syllables — a single breath of poetry. Every haiku carries a kigo, a season word (blossoms, cicadas, frost) that anchors it in time.

The best ones also hold a kireji, a cut: two images placed side by side so that the space between them sparks a small surprise. Concrete, present-tense, and about the world rather than the poet — that restraint is the whole art.
